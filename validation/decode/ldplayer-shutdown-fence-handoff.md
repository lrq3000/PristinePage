## Change and evidence contract

- Under the existing allocation lock, shutdown admits an explicitly `released` slot or an exact same-owner, same-generation `held` slot, such as a partial claim that has already captured both process identities. Nothing is written and no quit is dispatched when the slot belongs to another active generation. Reuse the unchanged exact inventory, PID/creation identity, targeted quit, `isrunning` and exit predicates; launch, claim and command I/O are unchanged.
- Persist pre-quit proof, then after verification persist the stopped inventory, exact `isrunning` response, and each original PID/start identity and exit observation. Flush the evidence files and request same-volume Windows write-through replacement. The durable manifest binds both proofs, the command transcript, runner and other retained evidence. Its record snapshot and `shutdown.json` are labeled `verified-before-release`; `usageReleased=false` is truthful at that checkpoint and is not later rewritten. The same-generation read-back of both released shared records proves actual release; only then does the success summary say `usageReleased=true`. The historical editor comparison verifier reads a pinned historical receipt and is unchanged; do not reuse its final `usageReleased` assertion for the new evidence stage.
- Keep the slot held until the instance release write succeeds. Record the exact admitted and intended record/slot snapshots so an acknowledgement failure after replacement cannot be mistaken for “no write.” Reconcile both independently under the lock without overwriting unfamiliar snapshots; restore the original reservation and identities where the instance record matches. The existing `shutdown_failed` and `released-with-unresolved-shutdown` states block admission. If neither blocker can be confirmed, retain the lock and report reconciliation required. This is a cooperative shared-lock fence, not an atomic transaction for non-cooperating writers or proof of real Windows storage behavior under power loss.
