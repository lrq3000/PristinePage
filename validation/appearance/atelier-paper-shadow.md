# Paper shadow correction — CARNET-SHADOW-01

T-07 / FR-06, after the geometry proposal work. [Paired results](atelier-document-color.md) show why an opt-in method for a sharp shadow is separate from the smooth Document color field. Do not advertise arbitrary shadow removal or reproduce a proprietary filter. Paper white, Natural lightness, Document color, and No enhancement retain their behavior and are not stacked with this single appearance stage.

`paper_shadow_v1` tests both axes independently in the stage input (already rectified and rotated). In at least 64 across-edge by 32 along-edge pixels, sample 13 equally spaced interior scanlines. Their column/row median encoded luma must show one adjacent step of at least 32, with a margin of `max(16, floor(across/6))` on both sides. Eleven scanlines must corroborate that step at the same index with the same sign and at least 28 levels of separation. Do not pick an axis if both support the hypothesis. The RGB references are the per-channel sample medians four pixels inside their respective regions. At each of five near/far sites in both regions at least ten scanlines must be within 12 levels per channel of that region's reference. The darker reference must have luma 98–192, the lighter 176–250 with no channel over 250, and neither reference can span more than 48 levels across channels. Each channel difference (lit minus dark) must be 20–115 and the range of those differences cannot exceed 30. Scan the full proposed boundary at two sites per side; three successive scanlines with the opposite paper color on either pair or loss of the correctly oriented 28-level step cause refusal. This conservative policy may also refuse a line interrupted by ink; it is not a semantic test for shadow.

Leave the lit side exactly unchanged. On the shaded side, with reference channels `D_c` and `L_c`, define the correction offset `delta=L_c−D_c` for `I_c≤D_c`. Above `D_c`, let `u=I_c−D_c`, `k=1/(255−L_c)−1/(255−D_c)`, and use `delta=L_c+u/(1+ku)−I_c` (nonnegative, reaches zero at 255). The target is continuous with slope one at the paper level and strictly increasing above it; the former abrupt highlight taper could erase or invert faint light marks. Let `e` be the maximum deviation from the shaded reference's three pairwise channel differences. Multiply the offset by `S(Y/Ydark,.55,.83)·(1−S(e,12,24))` with `S` the same smoothstep as Document color and round the candidate byte `I_c+delta·weight`. If any not-fully-protected shaded pixel brighter than the shaded reference would end up no brighter than the lit reference in byte-candidate luma, abandon the operation and retain the exact input. Otherwise use one ordinary strength/protection blend. The constant additive offset preserves faint paper-colored deficits; low lightness or a substantially different chroma reduces the correction. These checks do not prove perceptual fairness for all foreground colors or arbitrary protection fields. Weak/badly aligned evidence and zero strength retain exact pixels. A straight line joining two light paper colors may be indistinguishable from a shadow; inspect the preview or choose Original for colored stationery, prints and photographs.

Work is O(W×H) when correction is applied, with fixed-size scan windows and no full raster-valued field. Reserve both active RGB output arrays and fixed estimator workspace before stage execution. Check cancellation in the scan and output loops. Save the typed method/strength, not the inferred edge: preview and export can find different evidence at different raster sizes, with full-resolution quality and latency subject to separate testing. Originals, legacy algorithms, persistence schema, and permissions are unchanged.

On the paired hard-shadow fixture, Paper shadow removes the measured 75-level step entirely (0), retains the original 11-level faint-stroke contrast on both sides, leaves the lit RGB and the measured colored ink unchanged, and does nothing on the synthetic gradient. Core test coverage includes dark side reversal, transposition and saturated ink placed inside the shadow, rather than only on the unchanged lit side. Actual photographs, deliberately two-tone sheets, soft/irregular shadows and device costs still require separate evaluation.

Independent review identified two reproducible problems in the first implementation: positive faint marks lost or reversed contrast in the shaded region, and a step displaced over part of the image left a dark block. Both failed JUnit before the highlight shoulder and full-boundary consistency pass. Follow-up review reproduced a 13-level change that evaded the initial alignment veto and a faint tinted highlight that still reversed with chroma attenuation. Those and a saturated highlight veto fixture failed before strengthening the alignment and checking the actual rounded candidate. A successful no-op identifies execution of the requested stage, not positive detection; a dedicated no-evidence diagnostic remains a separate usability follow-up.

Final strict offline host verification reported 1,453 JVM cases across all five modules, zero failures/errors/skips, including six focused shadow cases and the codec, one-blend protection, carousel and independent batch save consumers. The 146 host SQLite cases passed. Both Android test targets compiled and app lint passed; no APK was assembled, signed, installed or executed in this continuation. The source-only follow-up confirmed disposition of the two concrete boundary/highlight findings and does not confer real-page or runtime acceptance.
